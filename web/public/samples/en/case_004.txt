Narrative
The patient is a 42-year-old woman with a 6-year history of major depressive disorder. On 2025-10-20 her treating psychiatrist prescribed DrugA, starting at 25 mg once daily and planning to increase to 50 mg once daily after one week if tolerated. The increase to 50 mg was made on or around 2025-10-27.

Her husband called the marketing authorization holder's hotline on 2025-12-10. He said his wife had "not been herself" since starting the new medicine and described a set of symptoms that had come on gradually and that he was concerned were related to the drug. He acknowledged that some of what he described might be part of her underlying depression, but said he called because of the timing relative to starting the medicine, and because she herself said the symptoms felt different from her previous experience of depression.

The patient was not interviewed directly for this report. With the patient's verbal consent, the company safety physician contacted the treating psychiatrist on 2025-12-12. The psychiatrist confirmed that the patient had reported similar symptoms at her most recent visit on 2025-12-05, and that treatment was being continued under close observation with a follow-up visit in two weeks. The psychiatrist agreed that the timing and symptom profile were consistent with possible adverse effects of DrugA, but stressed that stopping antidepressant therapy early carries clinical risks of its own.

At the time of this report the patient continues to take DrugA. Follow-up is planned.
