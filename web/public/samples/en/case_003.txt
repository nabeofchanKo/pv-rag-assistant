Narrative

A 74-year-old man with long-standing persistent atrial fibrillation, coronary artery disease, HFpEF, hypertension, CKD stage 3a, type 2 diabetes and mild cognitive impairment, under cardiovascular care at our institution. Since 2022 his atrial fibrillation had been managed with rate control (carvedilol) and anticoagulation with warfarin to a target INR of 2.0-3.0. On warfarin 3 mg/day his INR had been stable for the last 18 months.

In September 2025, because of worsening symptomatic palpitations and reduced exercise tolerance, a rhythm-control strategy was tried: DrugZ 200 mg twice daily was added as a 7-day loading regimen, then moved to 200 mg once daily for maintenance. The baseline ECG showed atrial fibrillation with a controlled ventricular response (78 bpm) and a QTc interval of 432 ms. Liver function, thyroid function and renal function were all within recent baseline ranges.

About one month after starting DrugZ (2025-10-12), routine INR monitoring showed an unexpected rise to 5.8, with no change in the warfarin dose, diet or other medication. The patient denied any new over-the-counter medicines, supplements or health foods, and had no febrile illness. Two days later (2025-10-14) he developed recurrent epistaxis requiring local compression and an outpatient visit. By 2025-10-15 he had extensive ecchymoses on both forearms after minor trauma. Warfarin was withheld for 2 days and then reduced from 3 mg to 1.5 mg/day, and the INR returned to range within a week. An interaction between DrugZ and warfarin through cytochrome P450 inhibition (particularly CYP2C9 and CYP3A4) was suspected.

The patient continued DrugZ at the 200 mg once-daily maintenance dose with closer warfarin monitoring. On 2025-11-22, about 10 weeks after starting DrugZ, he had a syncopal episode while walking from the bathroom and was brought to the emergency department by his daughter. On arrival his heart rate was 38 bpm and blood pressure 92/58 mmHg. The ECG showed atrial fibrillation with a markedly slowed ventricular response and a prolonged QTc interval (498 ms; baseline 432 ms). No proarrhythmia (torsade de pointes) was seen.

He was admitted for telemetry monitoring. Carvedilol and DrugZ were both withheld on admission. Over the next 48 hours the ventricular rate gradually recovered to 60-70 bpm and the QTc shortened to 460 ms, and he remained haemodynamically stable thereafter. Carvedilol was restarted at a reduced 3.125 mg twice daily without recurrence of bradycardia. The combination of DrugZ with existing beta-blocker therapy was thought to have caused excessive negative chronotropy, compounded by his age, reduced renal clearance and a possible interaction affecting carvedilol metabolism. Mild orthostatic hypotension was noted during the admission and had resolved by discharge.

After discharge on 2025-11-27, DrugZ remained stopped. Alternative antiarrhythmics and a return to pure rate control were discussed with the patient and his daughter; given his age, cognitive comorbidity and evident sensitivity to DrugZ, rate control was chosen.

On 2025-12-15, three weeks after DrugZ was stopped, his daughter reported new episodes of confusion, particularly from late afternoon into the evening; at times he did not recognize family members or know the date. He also complained of persistent mild dizziness. Although a temporal link with stopping rather than starting DrugZ is atypical, DrugZ has a long elimination half-life (estimated at several weeks for the active metabolite), and a delayed central nervous system effect could not be ruled out. His pre-existing mild cognitive impairment may also have been worsened by the recent admission and medication changes. Donepezil was continued unchanged.

By the date of this report (2026-01-20) the confusional episodes had gradually resolved over about 3 weeks, and the dizziness had also resolved. DrugZ remained stopped. INR was stable on warfarin 3 mg/day (the original dose, resumed after DrugZ washout). Heart rate was 68 bpm on carvedilol 3.125 mg twice daily. There was no recurrence of syncope or bleeding.

Drug-drug interaction discussion
The DrugZ-warfarin interaction seen here is consistent with the previously reported pharmacokinetic interaction mediated by cytochrome P450 inhibition. DrugZ is known to inhibit CYP2C9, the main enzyme metabolizing S-warfarin, increasing warfarin exposure and INR. Co-administration calls for close INR monitoring and a warfarin dose reduction, typically of 30-50%.

The bradycardia reflects additive negative chronotropy when DrugZ is combined with a beta-blocker in an elderly patient with reduced renal clearance.

Follow-up
A cardiology follow-up on 2026-02-15 is planned, with a repeat ECG and clinical assessment. DrugZ will not be re-administered. Alternative antiarrhythmics will be considered only if symptomatic palpitations recur.
