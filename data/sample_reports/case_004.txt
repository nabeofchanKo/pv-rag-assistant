NARRATIVE
The patient is a 42-year-old female with a 6-year history of major depressive disorder. She was prescribed DrugA by her treating psychiatrist on 2025-10-20, starting at 25 mg once daily, with a planned dose increase to 50 mg once daily after one week if tolerated. The dose increase to 50 mg occurred on or about 2025-10-27.

The report to the manufacturer's hotline was made by the patient's husband on 2025-12-10. The husband stated that his wife had been "different" since starting the new medication. He described a constellation of symptoms that had developed gradually and that he was concerned were related to the medication. The husband acknowledged that some of what he was describing might be part of his wife's underlying depression, but he stated that the timing of symptom onset relative to medication initiation, and his wife's own report that the symptoms felt qualitatively different from her usual experience of depression, prompted him to call.

The patient herself was not directly interviewed for this report. The treating psychiatrist was contacted by the company safety physician on 2025-12-12 with the patient's verbal consent. The psychiatrist confirmed that the patient had reported similar symptoms during her most recent visit on 2025-12-05 and that the medication was being continued under close observation with a follow-up visit planned in two weeks. The psychiatrist agreed that the temporal relationship and symptom profile were consistent with a possible adverse drug reaction to DrugA, but emphasised that premature discontinuation of antidepressant therapy carried its own clinical risk.

The patient continues to take DrugA at the time of this report. Follow-up is planned.
